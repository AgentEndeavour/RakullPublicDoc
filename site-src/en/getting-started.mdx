Raku is an **immersive Japanese reading platform**. Upload an article or a video and the app automatically splits it into sentences, translates it, and generates grammar explanations and audio narration. You read sentence by sentence in the reader and save sentences and words as reviewable cards.

This guide only covers **how to use the app's interface**; it does not cover installation or deployment.

## First launch

When you open the app, Raku runs in to greet you above the app name **Raku Learning Language** and two buttons. Choosing login or registration opens the form without the greeting animation:

- **Login** — you already have an account.
- **Register** — you are new and need an invite code to create an account.

Raku has **no username or password**: you always sign in with a third-party account or a passkey, so there is no extra password to remember.

## Sign in

On the welcome screen tap **Login**, check “**I have read and agree to the Privacy Policy**”, then pick a sign-in method:

- **Continue with Google**
- **Continue with GitHub**
- **Continue with WeChat** (only shown where the server has WeChat enabled)
- **Sign in with a passkey** (web / Android / Windows — see “Passkeys” below)
- Or tap **Continue without login** to browse as a guest (see “Browse without an account” below)

The login screen has **no invite-code field** and signing in does not require an invite code. After choosing your account on the authorization page you land on the main screen. The app **remembers your session**, so next time you open it you skip the login screen. You can **log out** from the account menu in the top-right corner.

<Note>
  If the third-party account you picked is **not registered yet**, you first see “**This account is not registered yet.**” Tap “**No account? Create one**”, enter a valid invite code, and finish registration **without another authorization redirect**. Do not use a sign-in method already tied to another account to register a new account — see the binding rules below.
</Note>

## Register a new account

New users join **by invitation** and need a valid **invite code** (ask an administrator or a user who has a personal invite code). On the welcome screen tap **Register**:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Enter an invite code">
    Type a valid invite code into the “Invite code” field. Invite codes are only entered during registration.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Agree to the privacy policy">
    Check “**I have read and agree to the Privacy Policy**”; tap “Privacy Policy” to read the full text.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose a provider and authorize">
    Tap “**Register with Google**” or “**Register with GitHub**” (and “**Register with WeChat**” where enabled), then complete authorization with that account.
  </Step>
</Steps>

With a valid invite code the account is **created and activated immediately** and you go straight to the main screen — there is no manual approval wait. An invalid or disabled code shows an error on the page; correct it and retry. Tap “**Have an account? Sign in**” at the bottom to return to login.

## How sign-in methods bind to accounts

One Raku account can be linked to **several** sign-in methods (one each of Google, GitHub and WeChat), but note:

<Note>
  **A single third-party account can belong to only one Raku account.** A WeChat / Google / GitHub identity already linked to one account cannot be used to register or link another account. The registration screen shows the same reminder: **if you already have an account, sign in directly** — reusing an old sign-in method to register a new account fails with an “already linked to another user” type of error.
</Note>

- Each sign-in method can be linked **at most once** per account (for example, you cannot link two Google accounts).
- To add more sign-in methods to your current account, sign in and open **Account settings** (see below).
- Ownership follows the authorized identity, so confirm you are using your own third-party account before linking.

## Manage sign-in methods in Account settings

In the account center, **Account & security** separates sign-in methods, passkeys, and invite codes. Sign-in cards show the provider icon, connection status, and linked email. They appear side by side on desktop and stack on narrow screens, using the same content width as the payment and ability pages.

After signing in, open the account menu in the top-right corner and go to **Account settings**, where you can:

- **Link** — associate a Google / GitHub / WeChat identity that is not used by another account (linking goes through one third-party authorization redirect).
- **Unlink** — remove a linked sign-in method.

Complete authorization and wait for Raku’s linking confirmation. A successful OAuth authorization alone does not confirm a saved Raku binding: check **Account → Account & security** for the provider’s **Unlink** action. Accounts with both Google and GitHub identities can link and sign in with either provider, regardless of which was used first.

When the “Set up sign-in methods” block appears during onboarding, each successful authorization returns to the same block with a compact, single-line green confirmation strip and a check-circle icon. The banner names the linked sign-in provider (for example, “GitHub linked”) and shows that provider account’s email when available. You can link another available provider there. The block also points to **Account → Account & security**, where you can manage and unlink sign-in methods later. The invite-code step appears after you dismiss the block; after the last provider is linked, passkey creation remains available when eligible. Close the block to continue.

When linking from **Account → Account & security**, authorization returns directly to that page, refreshes the linked identities, and displays the same green success strip within the page. Linking conflicts and failures also show their feedback there.

On supported platforms, after signing in with Google or GitHub, the same block also offers **Passkeys → Create**. Click it to open the system creation sheet and use your fingerprint, face, or device unlock on your next sign-in. The sheet never opens automatically. Success shows a green banner directly below the “Set up sign-in methods” title and replaces the action with a checkmark and “Set up”; cancellation leaves you free to retry or choose “Maybe later”. Existing passkeys show “Set up”, and users who already completed onboarding are not prompted again.

To keep you from locking yourself out, the app **does not let you unlink your last sign-in method** — an account must always keep at least one usable login (at least one third-party login; a passkey cannot be the only sign-in method).

## Passkeys

Selecting **Sign in with a passkey** first shows a reminder to turn off the VPN on your phone when scanning a QR code, as it may prevent the devices from connecting. Select **Continue sign-in** to start system verification, or cancel to stay on the login page.

A passkey is a **passwordless** sign-in method: on supported devices you can sign in with your **fingerprint, face scan, or security key** (such as Windows Hello), with no third-party authorization step.

Rules:

- **Sign-in only — passkeys cannot register a new account.** A passkey only resolves to an account that **already exists**.
- **Available on the web, the Android app, and the Windows app**; the entry is hidden in the native iOS / macOS apps (passkeys still work there when you open Raku in a browser).
- Before first use, **sign in once with Google or GitHub**, then create a passkey during onboarding or later under **Account settings → Passkeys**.
- Your system or chosen credential manager stores the passkey and may sync it to other devices; storage is not guaranteed to stay on this device alone.
- In Account settings you can **name / rename** passkeys, register more than one, and **delete** ones you no longer use.
- If a passkey matches no known account, you are told to sign in with Google or GitHub first.

## Browse without an account

After choosing Login on the welcome screen, the login screen has a “**Continue without login**” button at the bottom. Tap it to enter the app as a **guest**: you can browse public corpus and view existing translations, explanations, and grammar cards, and play any audio that already exists.

Guests **cannot** upload content, favorite articles or sentences, or add words to their vocabulary; these actions prompt you to log in.

## Switch the interface language

In the **top-right corner** of any page, click the language button (it shows the current language with a translate icon) to switch between **English / 简体中文 / 繁體中文 / 日本語**. Your choice is remembered for next time.

<Note>
  This switches the **interface language**; article translations and grammar explanations are generated by the backend and do not change with the interface language.
</Note>

## Assessment and progress reminders

If you have not completed an ability assessment, you receive one invitation after each successful login and may choose Maybe later. Refreshing or navigating does not repeat it. Completed assessments are checked against your account's server records, including on a new device.

Use Mark as studied in the reader toolbar to explicitly confirm that you have finished an article or book page. Each distinct material counts once; rereading, uploading, processing, or opening content does not increase the count. After 10 different articles or book pages newly completed on this device since your last assessment, you can receive a progress assessment invitation when confirming completion or returning to the study home. Choosing Maybe later postpones another invitation for at least seven days. Invitations respect the 24-hour assessment cooldown and preserve an assessment already in progress.

Your assessment result determines your level. A new completed assessment starts a new accumulation period. Completion records stay on this device, scoped to your account, and do not automatically sync across devices. Clearing device learning memory also clears these records. Unavailable assessment services do not treat an unknown status as never assessed or block entry to the app.
