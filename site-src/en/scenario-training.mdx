The **Scenario Training** tab puts you inside an interactive story where you **unlock your own travel route through real causes and effects**. Every choice you make in Japanese — transport, asking directions, checking luggage, eating, checking in — shapes how the story unfolds.

## Japan Trip Simulator

The current story collection is the **Japan Trip Simulator**: a five-day story of your first solo trip to Japan, from arriving in Tokyo and exploring the city to a nearby excursion and finally flying home. Each page presents one story cover, with its title, introduction, progress (e.g. “0 of 5 days complete”), and entry button at the lower left. When several stories are available, swipe horizontally or use the bottom arrow buttons; focus the gallery to use the keyboard arrow keys. The counter shows your position. A single story has no paging controls.

Click **Start story** to enter the five-day itinerary. An existing journey shows **Continue story**; a completed story offers **View the complete journey**. Returning from the journey refreshes progress while keeping the selected story.

## The five days

The story uses a timeline (arranged vertically on phones) to show the five days: the current day is highlighted, later days show “locked”, and you can only enter the next day after completing the previous one.

Each day's page contains:

| Module | Contents |
|---|---|
| **Story background** | The time, place, and situation of the day (e.g. “On the morning of 2026-10-16, you arrive alone in Japan for the first time”) |
| **Today's schedule** | The tasks to complete in chronological order |
| **Today's goals** | The key outcomes you must achieve (e.g. getting your room key) |
| **Travel tips** | Practical reminders about transport, luggage, payment, and more |

## Start today's journey

Click **Start today's journey** to enter the day's concrete scenes: you use Japanese to communicate with staff at stations, hotels, and restaurants, handling real tasks such as transport, directions, luggage, meals, and check-in. Your expressions are understood and scored, moving the story forward. After completing a day you unlock the next, until day five when you fly home.

On phones, drag the map with one finger and pinch with two fingers to zoom. Tap the current node to open its task or a completed node to review it; unvisited branch previews cannot be entered yet. A travel illustration marks your current location. Detail images use compact previews; tap a picture to view it full size.

<Tip>
  It helps to build vocabulary and patterns in [Deep Reading](/user-guide/library) first, then get “real practice” in Scenario Training. You can also flip through the [Grammar Cards](/user-guide/grammar) whenever an expression is uncertain.
</Tip>


## Ability adaptation inside conversations

Simulation and reading use the same ability evidence to adjust NPC language, feedback depth and reference explanations. Story objectives and completion rules remain intact, and help remains available. Independent utterances can add grammar/vocabulary production evidence; copied examples, help requests and text answers do not establish independent mastery or listening. Reference explanations are generated when individual notes are expanded; unknown points remain explained.
