The **Interest Plaza** tab at the far right of the top navigation is where you discover and join **interest groups**. Guests can browse the plaza freely; joining and creating groups require an account.

## Browse and filter groups

The plaza lists every **active, public** group as a card. Each card shows:

- The **group name** and **description**
- **Tags** (e.g. anime, music)
- The **member count** and **featured article count**
- A preview of featured article titles

Use the **search box** to find groups by name, or the **tag filter bar** to see only groups for a specific interest. Pull down to refresh.

> **Private groups never appear on the plaza** — non-members cannot search for them or open their detail pages. Only members can see them (see [Public vs private](#public-vs-private-groups)).

## Joining a group

There are two ways:

1. **Join by share code**: get the group's share code from a member and choose "Join by code". You become a member immediately, no approval needed.
2. **Request to join**: open the group detail page and choose "Request to join", optionally with a message. The group Owner or an admin must approve your request. While waiting, the page shows a pending status.

## Creating a group

Anyone can choose "Create a group" and submit an application with:

- A **group name** (2–40 characters, unique across the site)
- A **description**
- The **main interest**
- **1–6 tags**
- A description of the **planned content**
- **Visibility**: public (default) or private

The group goes live only after a site administrator approves it. If your application is rejected, you can see the reason under "My applications".

## Public vs private groups

- **Public (default)**: listed on the Interest Plaza; anyone can browse and request to join.
- **Private**: absent from the plaza, search, and tag filters. Non-members cannot tell the group exists or request to join; new members can only be added directly with the group's **share code**.
- Either way, a group always appears in its **members'** own group list and in the home "Interest picks" panel — visibility only affects public plaza exposure, never what members see.
- Owners/admins can switch between public and private at any time on the [group management](#managing-your-groups) page. The change takes effect immediately and never affects existing members.

## Official weekly history

The **Official weekly history** block at the top of the plaza shows the full history of weekly picks (both currently featured and removed ones) in a horizontal row. Tap a card to open the article.

## Group detail

Tap any group card to open its detail page. You can see the description, tags, **share code**, the Owner/admin list, and the featured article titles:

- **Members**: tap a title to read the article.
- **Non-members**: titles are locked until you join the group.

## Managing your groups

Choose "Manage my groups" on the plaza and pick a group you belong to open its management page. Members can apply to become an admin. Owners and admins can:

- View, copy, or **regenerate the invite code** (the group's share code — after regenerating, the previous code stops working immediately); share it with friends so they can join directly
- Switch the group between **public and private** at any time (private hides it from the plaza; members only)
- Review **join requests** (only public groups accept requests; private groups are joinable by share code only)
- Run the **first-stage review** of articles recommended by members
- **Recommend an article** to the group
- **Remove members** (only an Owner can remove an admin)

Owners can additionally approve **admin promotion requests** or **dissolve the group**. Regular members can **leave** the group.

> Group articles go through two stages — group first-stage review, then a site administrator's final review. Once approved they are visible to members only and the article's original visibility is unchanged.

Below "Weekly recommendations" on the home page, the **My interest groups** panel aggregates approved articles from your groups and labels each with its source group.
