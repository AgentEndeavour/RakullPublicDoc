The info area at the top shows the **analysis model** — it comes from the AI call that actually completed the translation and explanation for this article, not the model selected at upload time. Older articles with no call history show “no record”.

## Read a book continuously

Entering from a processed page in a book's quick view keeps the whole book's source in the reader and automatically reveals the page you selected. On wide screens, scroll continuously through the source pane on the left, without switching between separate readers for individual pages.

Select a sentence on another processed page to show its translation and grammar explanation in the same view. Audio, saved sentences and notes always belong to the original sentence on the selected page. Unparsed sections retain their original text and show an explicit unparsed status when selected; processing and failed pages show their respective states. Browsing and selecting text do not start processing or incur processing charges.

## Manage your bookshelf

After signing in, click **Manage** (the pencil icon) beside **Refresh** in the bookshelf header, then click books or their checkboxes to select them. **Select all** selects books in the current category. Click **Delete** and confirm to remove selected books; cancelling the confirmation deletes nothing. Books that fail to delete remain available for retry. Click **Cancel** to leave management mode and open books for reading again. The same controls are available on computers and phones.

## Read sentence by sentence

On a wide screen the reader has three panes: **Japanese source / Grammar explanation / Chinese translation**. On a phone, each sentence is shown as a bilingual card. The source keeps its natural paragraph layout, but every sentence can be selected independently:

- Click any sentence and it is highlighted in both the source and the translation, while the **grammar** pane updates with that sentence's explanation.
- A sentence already in view is not force-centred; source and translation stay in sync per sentence and do not jump back to the paragraph start even when the panes differ in height.

### Look up words

Hovering a word shows a light highlight; the first click on a word in an unselected sentence only selects the whole sentence, and a second click on the word opens a **word card**. From the word card you can “**Add to vocabulary**”; the button then becomes “Added to vocabulary” in place, and clicking again removes it (see [Favorites and review](/user-guide/collections)).

## The sentence study panel

After selecting a sentence, the grammar pane organizes everything there is to learn about it:

| Module | Contents |
|---|---|
| **Source** | The full sentence (selectable for copying), the **play button**, and **save this sentence** |
| **Translation** | The sentence translation |
| **Grammar** | The grammar explanation; keywords such as verb conjugations can be clicked to open the matching rule card |
| **Note** | Write and save your own note |

Click the **speaker** in the source module to hear the sentence read aloud. The bookmark in the top toolbar favorites the **whole article**, while “save this sentence” stores it as a sentence card.

## Select and copy text

On a computer you can drag to select text in the source or translation pane, across sentences and paragraphs, then press `Cmd+C` (macOS) / `Ctrl+C` (Windows). The two panes select independently without mixing languages. Copying the source includes only the text, without furigana or romaji.

On a phone, long-press the source or translation of a sentence card, drag the handles to adjust the range, and then tap “Copy”. The range is limited to one language in the current card.


## Rate translations and grammar explanations

Both panes have three rating icons at the bottom right: **thumb down = Poor, thumb up = Good, two thumbs up = Very good**. In the wide layout, the translation rating covers the article translation and the grammar rating covers the currently displayed sentence explanation. Ratings in mobile sentence cards cover that sentence. Sign in to submit feedback. Icons are outlined until a successful rating fills the selected icon; Very good uses two overlapping, offset thumbs.

Select translation or grammar text with your mouse to open a horizontal toolbar above the selection. Choose **Rate**, then one of the same three icons to rate the selected text. Copy remains available. Translation selections can span sentences and paragraphs; vertical translations also support mouse dragging.

After a successful overall rating, a dismissible tip above the buttons explains that you can select a specific sentence for more precise feedback. It appears once per reader session and is not triggered by a selection rating. Failed submissions show an error and can be retried.

Ratings are stored in the database with the user, article, module, score, selected text, sentence location, content snapshot, and submission time. Administrators can view them under **Admin → User feedback → Reader ratings**, filter by module or score, and load more records. Expand a record to see its selection, source context, and content snapshot. Other users cannot read these records.

## Grammar reference cards

Both the grammar pane and the top toolbar have a **book** button that opens the unified **grammar library** (755 N5–N1 cards). You can also open **JLPT Grammar · Lanbaoshu** from the **Kotonoha** bookshelf. See [Grammar Cards](/user-guide/grammar) for details.

## Play the whole article

Click **Play all** at the top of the reader to read aloud in order, starting from the first sentence that already has audio. During playback you can pause, resume, or stop, and the current and total sentence counts are shown. Missing or failed audio is skipped automatically; the sentence being read gets a light highlight without changing your selected sentence.

## Failed audio and retries

When the text is processed successfully but some audio fails, the task shows “partially completed”, and existing translations, explanations, and successful audio can still be read normally. The reader distinguishes between no audio yet, failed generation, TTS not configured, and network errors: you can retry a single sentence or use “regenerate missing audio” in bulk.

## Manage your articles

If you uploaded the article (administrators can manage all articles), the reader offers extra actions:

- **Edit**: change the title, content, etc.
- **Reparse**: re-split and regenerate explanations.
- **Make public / private**: toggle whether others can see it.
- **Delete**: delete the article; this **cannot be undone**.
- Within a sentence you can also **regenerate the explanation**.

### Opening the mobile reader

Each time you open the mobile reader, a “Using the mobile reader” dialog states that mobile defaults to sentence-by-sentence reading: original text, translation, then grammar. Three equally styled, clearly separated blocks illustrate this order, alongside the reading tools and a short scrolling hint. The first sentence’s grammar is shown on entry; selecting another sentence switches the explanation. The mobile browser and app use the same guide.

The guide appears on first use. “Don’t show again” starts unchecked; select it and press “Got it, start reading” to stop automatic prompts. Confirming without checking it, closing the dialog, or tapping outside it keeps prompts enabled for subsequent visits. This preference is local to the current browser or app; clearing its data restores prompts. Use the question-mark button in the toolbar to reopen the guide and change this preference.

## Explanations that adapt to your ability

Choose an initial reading self-check preference before processing. While reading, use the compact **Self-check** switch beside **?**; the group starts at the bottom left and can be dragged within the screen. It overlays content without a bottom bar, keeping translation ratings visible at the bottom right. Changes apply to the current page immediately, without reprocessing or added fees; a completed ability assessment is required to enable checks. The information button explains eligibility and offers assessment or retry actions. With checks off, translations and explanations remain visible in full.

No N1–N5 explanation setting is required. Relevant device evidence guides teaching for weak/unknown points, star-masked recall questions and less repetition of well-learned content. Show full explanation opens a reference dialog rather than a self-check answer mask. Revealing is not correctness; feedback updates only the tested point.

Account Center → Personal ability retains listening, speaking, reading, writing and life in Japan. Assessment details retain vocabulary, grammar, listening, kanji and reading. Each dimension uses its own response evidence; vocabulary and grammar choices do not become speaking or writing scores.

The check has at most ten response tasks. It first covers each domain, then uses actual answers to select follow-up questions expected to clarify uncertain boundaries. Tasks include vocabulary, grammar, listening, kanji, passage comprehension, daily situations, Japanese writing and spoken recordings. Type your written response; upload your own spoken response as an MP3, WAV, M4A, WebM, Ogg or AAC file up to 5 MB. Audio is transcribed to evaluate this sample of language production; it does not provide phonetic pronunciation, intonation or acoustic fluency scoring.

When grading is unavailable, your current response remains available for retry, without counting a service failure as a wrong answer. Writing and speaking tasks can also be skipped unscored. Missing dimensions keep their axes and remain explicitly unmeasured. Results show valid question counts and tentative ranges, rather than issuing an overall level from the hardest question answered correctly.

After completion, select **Retake assessment** to start another full session through the new assessment API without waiting for the daily cooldown. Previous results and certificates remain available; existing rewards are not granted again. Unseen questions are preferred, with cross-session reuse when needed and no repeated item within one session.

Each domain has only a small sample. The model and item difficulties have not been calibrated against real learners, so ten tasks cannot promise precise measurement of full ability. Raw success rates do not trigger early completion. Older records are retained, but their single level is no longer treated as reliable overall placement. Everyday learning continues to supply evidence; optional follow-up invitations appear only at learning boundaries. Memory is stored locally, with necessary context sent for personalized processing and no automatic cross-device synchronization.

Personal ability displays issued learning certificates as full artwork. Select **View certificate** for a zoomable full-screen preview, or **Save certificate** to export a high-resolution PNG. Each includes the account name, achievement, issue date and certificate number as an in-app learning record.

Reparse opens settings with reading self-checks off by default. With checks off, translations and explanations remain visible and no completed assessment is required. Enabling checks requires a completed ability assessment. Each check shows a specific question before its reference answer is revealed; ordinary explanations without a question are never hidden as a self-check. Spoiler particles drift continuously unless the system requests reduced motion.
